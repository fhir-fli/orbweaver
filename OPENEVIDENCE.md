## OPEN — cervical-spine imaging rules for a low-resource bedside reference (2026-09-07)

A bedside reference used at a district hospital in Gulu, northern Uganda (low-resource,
paediatric-heavy; plain radiography usually available, CT often not) already carries the
NEXUS low-risk criteria as a five-item checklist (any item present means image) and the
PECARN rule for head CT in children. Should it also carry the Canadian C-Spine Rule, and how?

1. In alert (GCS 15), haemodynamically stable blunt-trauma patients aged 16 and over, do
   current guidelines (ACEP, EAST, NICE, or others) prefer the Canadian C-Spine Rule over
   NEXUS? The one prospective head-to-head reported 99.4% versus 90.7% sensitivity and 45.1%
   versus 36.8% specificity in favour of the Canadian rule, but it was run by that rule's
   developers and clinicians skipped the range-of-motion step in 10.2% of patients.
2. State the Canadian C-Spine Rule exactly as published: each high-risk factor, each
   dangerous-mechanism component (fall height, speed, vehicle types), each low-risk factor,
   the range-of-motion threshold, and every exclusion criterion, one per line.
3. Should the NEXUS entry carry caveats that sensitivity is lower in patients over 65 and
   that its paediatric validation enrolled only 88 children under 2 years, none injured?
4. Where CT is unavailable, is "rule negative, or rule positive with normal three-view
   plain films" an acceptable clearance pathway, and what wording should the reference use
   for a rule-positive patient whose plain films are normal?
