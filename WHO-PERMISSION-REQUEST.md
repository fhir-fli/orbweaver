## §11 · Where WHO's material will be used

The material will be used in Orbweaver, a free offline clinical reference application for Android and iOS, written for health workers in low-resource district hospitals. The Pocket book is rendered as 259 topics laid out for a phone screen, so a clinician can find the guidance for the child in front of them without carrying the printed book. Navigation follows the book's own chapter and section structure.

Thirteen of the manual-technique line drawings are reproduced as printed: back slaps, chest thrusts, the Heimlich manoeuvre, neutral and tilting airway positions, look-listen-feel, jaw thrust, bag-and-mask ventilation, nasal prongs, nasal catheter, cervical stabilisation and the recovery position. They are reproduced because the technique cannot be learned from a sentence.

The text is restructured rather than copied page for page, and a small number of figures have been updated against current evidence, with each change and its source recorded on the page it affects. WHO is credited as the source on an in-app credits screen, which states that WHO is not responsible for any changes made to the original content and that responsibility for the adaptation rests with the publisher of the application.

The application requires no network connection, collects no data of any kind, and contains no advertising, no analytics and no in-app purchases. It is not sold. Everything it displays is bundled with it and read from the device.

The applicant is a physician boarded in internal medicine, paediatrics and clinical informatics who has practised in the settings this book is written for, including a year as an attending in paediatrics and internal medicine at a regional referral hospital in northern Uganda.

## §12 · How are you planning to distribute your material, and to whom?

Distribution is through the Google Play and Apple App Store closed-testing channels, Play closed testing and Apple TestFlight, to a small named group of clinicians invited by the applicant, for evaluation and feedback. The application is free. There is no paid tier, no advertising and no in-app purchase.

If it goes on to a public listing it will remain free, with no advertising and no data collection, and the intended audience is health workers in low-resource district hospitals of the kind this book addresses.
