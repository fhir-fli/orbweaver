# WHO licence — GRANTED, request ID 202610340

Granted 2026-09-03 by the WHO Permissions Team. A non-exclusive, worldwide,
royalty-free, non-transferable, non-sublicensable licence to reproduce the
Pocket book of hospital care for children, 2nd ed., and incorporate it in this
application, covering current and future releases of the app.

The request asked for scope covering both closed testing and a later free public
listing. It was granted as submitted, so **no further request is needed at public
release**. A *future edition* of the Pocket book would require a new licence.

Superseded: the earlier note that permission was still needed. Do not re-raise it.

## What the licence requires of the app

These are conditions, not suggestions. Breach terminates the licence automatically.

**Clause 3 — the app must say it is not WHO's.** It must be clear to the viewer
that the product was not developed or published by WHO and should not be credited
to WHO, with a bibliographic reference to the source. ✅ Applied: the credits
screen now opens with exactly that statement and carries the full citation and the
request ID.

**Clause 9 — no use of the WHO name or emblem, or any abbreviation.** ✅ Applied:
the book's shelf subtitle was `WHO · IMCI 2nd ed.` and is now `Pocket Book · 2nd
ed.`. ⚠️ **This sits in tension with clause 3, which requires an acknowledgement
that necessarily names WHO.** The reading applied: cite the source where a
citation belongs — the credits screen — and do not use the name as a label on a
book cover, which is nearer to branding. If that reading is wrong it is one line
to reverse, and the reasoning is recorded in `book.yaml` beside the change.

**Clause 8 — no advertising anywhere in the app, and no implication of WHO
endorsement.** ✅ Holds: the app has no advertising, no analytics and no network
code at all.

**Clause 5 — no company or product branding may be added to the licensed
material**, and modification beyond matching the app's format or style needs
written authorisation. ⚠️ **We update some figures against current evidence.**
That was disclosed verbatim in the request and granted on those representations,
so it is covered — but it is covered *because it was disclosed*. Any new class of
change should be checked against this clause.

**Clause 7 — factual and appropriate context only**, no marketing or promotional
use. ✅ Holds.

**Clause 11 — indemnity.** The app's publisher indemnifies WHO against claims
arising from this use.

**Clause 6 — AI.** Not currently engaged; the app has no AI features. If any are
added, the licensed material may be used for them but must not be released into
public LLMs or made available for unrestricted reuse, verbatim excerpts must be
attributed with a reference, generated text must NOT be attributed to WHO, and the
app must tell users that diagnosis and treatment remain the clinician's
responsibility.

## Third-party material

Clause 1 covers only material belonging to WHO. Anything inside the publication
credited to another source is excluded and is the licensee's responsibility. The
request recorded that a search of the full text of all 438 pages for attribution
phrases returned no results, and that the acknowledgements name contributors,
reviewing institutions and funders rather than copyright holders. That search
covered the PDF's text layer, so a credit printed inside an image would not have
appeared in it.
