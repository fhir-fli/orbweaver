## Q1 · One new formulary entry to check — amoxicillin–clavulanate, paediatric

A paediatric hospital reference names **oral amoxicillin–clavulanate as the FIRST choice**
for a urinary tract infection in a child with fever, systemic upset, signs of
pyelonephritis, or in an infant, and mentions it again for protracted bacterial bronchitis.
Its drug annex has **no entry for it at all**, so a clinician looking the drug up finds
nothing. The entry below was written from the dose the treatment page itself gives.

**Entry as written:**

> **Amoxicillin–clavulanate (co-amoxiclav)** — penicillin with a beta-lactamase inhibitor.
> **Urinary tract infection, first choice:** oral **25–50 mg/kg/day of the amoxicillin
> component**, in 2–3 divided doses. Dose and prescribe by the amoxicillin component,
> never by the combined figure.

**Questions:**

1. Is 25–50 mg/kg/day of the amoxicillin component, in 2–3 divided doses, correct for a
   child with a febrile urinary tract infection? If not, give the correct figure as a bare
   number range on its own line with no citation marker.

2. Is there a maximum daily dose, and does the clavulanate component impose its own
   ceiling? Give any number alone, or write "none".

3. Should a duration be printed with it, and if so how many days for a febrile urinary
   tract infection in a child? A number alone on its own line.

## Q2 · Hypoglycaemia cutoff in a child with malaria — 2.2 or 2.5 mmol/L

Two paediatric hospital references disagree on the blood glucose below which a child
with malaria is treated for hypoglycaemia.

One reference's malaria page states:

> Hypoglycemia (< 2.2 mmol/L)

The second reference uses a higher figure in three separate places — its severe
malaria page, its bedside blood-glucose page, and its coma and convulsions table:

> Blood glucose < 2.5 mmol/L (< 45 mg/dL) — or < 3.0 mmol/L (< 54 mg/dL) in severe
> malnutrition — is hypoglycaemia: treat at once.

> hypoglycaemia — blood glucose < 2.5 mmol/L (< 45 mg/dl); check in all children with
> signs of severe malaria

The first reference has no other general hypoglycaemia figure anywhere. Its only other
threshold is < 3 mmol/L on its severe acute malnutrition page, which matches the second
reference's malnutrition figure.

So a child with severe malaria and a glucose of 2.3 mmol/L is treated by one reference
and not by the other.

**Questions:**

1. In a child with malaria, what blood glucose defines hypoglycaemia requiring
   immediate treatment? Give the figure as a bare number on its own line, in mmol/L,
   with no citation marker.

2. Does that figure change in a child who also has severe acute malnutrition? Give
   that number alone on its own line, or write "same".

3. Is there a separate, lower figure that applies only to a symptomatic child, or only
   to a neonate? Give any such number alone on its own line, or write "none".

## Q3 · CD4 percentage as the immunosuppression threshold — 14% or 15%

Two paediatric hospital references use different CD4 percentages for severe
immunosuppression in HIV.

One reference, on a page covering both adults and children, states:

> PCP / toxoplasma encephalitis — CD4 < 200 or %CD4 < 14% (and for toxoplasma when
> CD4 < 100 and toxoplasma IgG positive)

The second reference uses 15% throughout:

> Infected, age ≥ 6 years: may discontinue after ≥ 6 months on ART with CD4 ≥ 15% or
> ≥ 200/mm³ for > 3 consecutive months

> Yellow fever vaccine is a live vaccine — contraindicated in children with
> symptomatic HIV or CD4 < 15%.

> Varicella (VZV-seronegative, 1–8 yr): give if CD4 > 15%

**Questions:**

1. For a CHILD, what CD4 percentage marks severe immunosuppression for the purpose of
   starting co-trimoxazole prophylaxis against Pneumocystis? Give the number as a bare
   percentage on its own line with no citation marker.

2. Is the figure different for an adult or adolescent? Give that number alone on its
   own line, or write "same".

3. Below what age is CD4 percentage used instead of an absolute CD4 count, and does an
   absolute count threshold apply above that age? Give the age and any count as bare
   numbers on their own lines.
