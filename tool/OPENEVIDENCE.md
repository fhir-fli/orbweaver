## Q1 · Hypoglycaemia outside malaria — should the general threshold be 3.0 too?

A previous ruling raised the treatment threshold for hypoglycaemia **in a child with
malaria** to 3.0 mmol/L, and both references now print 3.0 on their malaria pages. The
same figure is already used for a child with severe acute malnutrition.

One of the references still prints a **lower** figure on its general pages — the bedside
blood-glucose page, the coma and convulsions differential table, and the IV glucose
chart:

> Blood glucose < 2.5 mmol/L (< 45 mg/dL) — or < 3.0 mmol/L (< 54 mg/dL) in severe
> malnutrition — is hypoglycaemia: treat at once.

So a child now has hypoglycaemia treated at 3.0 on the malaria pathway and at 2.5 on
the general pathway, in the same book.

The setting is a district hospital in northern Uganda: low-resource,
paediatric-heavy, malaria-endemic, limited drug availability, no reliable dialysis, ICU
monitoring not routinely available, and **a repeat glucose measurement may be delayed
or unavailable**.

**Questions:**

   also be 3.0 mmol/L? Answer yes or no on its own line, and give the figure as a bare
   number on its own line with no citation marker.

2. If it should stay lower for a child who is not sick, what is the figure, and what
   distinguishes the two situations in one sentence a clinician can apply at the
   bedside?

3. Do the staged neonatal targets (maintain above 2.8 mmol/L in the first 48 hours,
   above 3.3 after, above 3.9 in a persistent hypoglycaemia disorder) stand unchanged?
