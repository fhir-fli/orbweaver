## Q1 · Phenobarbital loading dose in a convulsing child — 15 mg/kg or 20 mg/kg?

One reference gives, on its drug-formulary page:

> **Phenobarbitone / Phenobarbital.** Neonates: loading 20 mg/kg IV/IM, maintenance
> 5 mg/kg IV/IM/PO once daily. Infants / Children / Adults: **loading 15 mg/kg IV/IM
> (max initial loading dose 320 mg)**, maintenance 5 mg/kg IV/IM/PO once daily. Give
> over 20 minutes if IV, not faster than 1 mg/kg/min.

The same reference gives, on its resuscitation-medications page, for both its paediatric
and its adult table:

> **Load 20 mg/kg IM/IV over 20 min (all ages).** If still fitting after 15 min give a
> further 10 mg/kg, to a cumulative max 40 mg/kg — only where respiratory monitoring is
> available. Maintenance 3-5 mg/kg IM/IV/PO once daily.

A second, paediatric-only reference gives **20 mg/kg** for neonates and low-birth-weight
infants, and separately **15 mg/kg IM/IV over 15 minutes** for an older child in status
epilepticus after two doses of diazepam have failed.

**The concern:** this is the drug given when diazepam has failed, so the patient is still
fitting while the dose is being looked up. The two pages of the same reference disagree
for the same patient, and they are not reconcilable by adding a cap: 320 mg is reached at
16 kg on the 20 mg/kg scheme, so an adult would be capped at well under half the other
page's dose.

**Questions:**

1. For a child aged over 1 month in status epilepticus, after two doses of a
   benzodiazepine have failed, what is the phenobarbital loading dose in mg/kg? Please
   give it as a bare number on its own line with no citation marker.

2. For an adult in the same situation, what is the loading dose in mg/kg? A bare number
   on its own line.

3. Is there a maximum initial loading dose in milligrams, and if so what is it? Give the
   number alone, or write "none".

4. Is the maintenance dose 3-5 mg/kg/day or 5 mg/kg/day?

5. Is a cumulative maximum of 40 mg/kg correct, and does it apply at all ages?
