## Q1 · One new formulary entry to check — amoxicillin–clavulanate, paediatric

A paediatric hospital reference names **oral amoxicillin–clavulanate as the FIRST choice**
for a urinary tract infection in a child with fever, systemic upset, signs of
pyelonephritis, or in an infant, and mentions it again for protracted bacterial bronchitis.
Its drug annex has **no entry for it at all**, so a clinician looking the drug up finds
nothing. The entry below was written from the dose the treatment page itself gives.

**Entry as written:**

> **Amoxicillin–clavulanate (co-amoxiclav)** — penicillin with a beta-lactamase inhibitor.
> **Urinary tract infection, first choice:** oral **25–50 mg/kg/day of the amoxicillin
> component**, in 2–3 divided doses. Dose and prescribe by the amoxicillin component,
> never by the combined figure.

**Questions:**

1. Is 25–50 mg/kg/day of the amoxicillin component, in 2–3 divided doses, correct for a
   child with a febrile urinary tract infection? If not, give the correct figure as a bare
   number range on its own line with no citation marker.

2. Is there a maximum daily dose, and does the clavulanate component impose its own
   ceiling? Give any number alone, or write "none".

3. Should a duration be printed with it, and if so how many days for a febrile urinary
   tract infection in a child? A number alone on its own line.
