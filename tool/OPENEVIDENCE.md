## Q1 · Dihydroartemisinin–piperaquine — is the piperaquine target dose the same mg/kg at every weight?

A paediatric reference prints:

> Dihydroartemisinin plus piperaquine. Fixed-dose combination in tablets containing 40 mg
> dihydroartemisinin and 320 mg piperaquine. Aim for a target dose of 4 mg/kg per day
> dihydroartemisinin and **18 mg/kg per day piperaquine** once a day for 3 days.

Its own drug formulary, for the same combination, prints:

> Oral once daily for 3 days: dihydroartemisinin ~4 mg/kg + piperaquine **~24 mg/kg** per
> day (higher mg/kg target in children under 25 kg).

**Questions:**

1. What is the target piperaquine dose in mg/kg per day for a child under 25 kg? Give the
   number alone on its own line, with no citation marker.

2. What is it for a patient of 25 kg or more? A number alone on its own line.

3. If the target is a single figure at all weights, say which figure, and confirm that
   the weight-band tablet counts are what produce any apparent difference in mg/kg.

## Q2 · Ferrous sulfate — does a 200 mg or a 325 mg tablet contain 65 mg of elemental iron?

One page of a reference prints:

> Adults: 65 mg once daily of elemental iron (= ferrous sulfate 200 mg, one tablet).

The drug formulary of the same reference prints:

> 325 mg ferrous sulfate tablet contains 65 mg elemental iron.

Both cannot describe the same tablet. A clinician who takes the wrong one is out by about
40% on every dose, in a setting where iron deficiency anaemia is extremely common.

**Questions:**

1. How many milligrams of elemental iron are in a 200 mg ferrous sulfate tablet? A number
   alone on its own line.

2. How many are in a 325 mg ferrous sulfate tablet? A number alone on its own line.

3. If the answer depends on whether the salt is dried/exsiccated or the heptahydrate,
   say so explicitly and give the elemental iron content of each.

## Q3 · Lidocaine — confirm the maximum infiltration doses for a child

A paediatric reference's drug annex prints only "Local injection: 4–5 mg/kg per dose as
local anaesthetic", with no mention of whether the solution contains epinephrine. Its
procedural pages use a conservative ceiling of 3 mg/kg.

The formulary entry has been rewritten to read: **plain, maximum 4.5 mg/kg; with
epinephrine, maximum 7 mg/kg**, keeping the 3 mg/kg conservative ceiling for procedures.

**Questions:**

1. What is the maximum infiltration dose of PLAIN lidocaine in a child, in mg/kg? A
   number alone on its own line.

2. What is it WITH epinephrine, in mg/kg? A number alone on its own line.

3. Is a single maximum of "4–5 mg/kg" without stating the preparation safe to print in a
   drug formulary, or should the two be separated? Yes/no and one sentence.
