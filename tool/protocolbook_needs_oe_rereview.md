# 1. CONFLICTS — research contradicts OE's first answer

## 1.1 🔴 N-acetylcysteine: 2-bag for everyone, or 3-bag under 40 kg?
- **OE:** 2-bag >40 kg; keep 3-bag for ≤40 kg (FDA caution). **← APPLIED**
- **Research:** 2-bag for **everyone**, weight-scaled: bag 1 = 200 mg/kg in
  **7 mL/kg up to 500 mL** over 4 h; bag 2 = 100 mg/kg in **14 mL/kg up to
  1000 mL** over 16 h (ANZ 2020).
- **The point neither of us raised:** the 3-bag regimen's *fixed adult diluent
  volumes* in a small child risk **hyponatraemia** — which cuts against OE's
  paediatric caution, in a paediatric-heavy service.
- 2NAC (n=2,211): non-inferior for liver injury; anaphylactoid reactions
  **7.1% → 1.3%**, p<0.0001. 2025 *Clin Toxicol* meta-analysis agrees.
- **ASK:** does the hyponatraemia risk outweigh the FDA's ≤40 kg caution here?

## 1.2 🔴 Paediatric atropine in OP poisoning: 0.02 or 0.05 mg/kg?
- **OE:** 0.02 mg/kg confirmed, no change. **← currently unchanged**
- **Research:** **UCG 2023 prints 0.05 mg/kg**, doubling q3–5 min. IPCS/WHO
  0.015–0.05; MSF 0.02–0.05. 0.02 is the *bottom of a range*, and the national
  guideline says 0.05.
- **Both agree:** print **no minimum absolute dose** (the 0.1 mg floor traces to
  an unsupported sentence about a 1971 paper — Barrington, *Pediatrics* 2011).
- **Not on our page, should be:** titrate to **secretions**, not heart rate —
  "do not stop atropine for tachycardia, especially in children."
- **ASK:** match UCG at 0.05 mg/kg?

## 1.3 🟡 Pralidoxime: omit silently, or omit with a footnote?
- **OE:** adding it is reasonable AND omitting it is defensible — equivocal.
- **Research:** omit, firmly. Eddleston 2009 (placebo-controlled, WHO dose, with
  red-cell AChE proving target engagement): mortality **24.8% vs 15.8% against**.
  Cochrane 2011: WHO regimen "is not supported." Cureus 2020: intermediate
  syndrome **significantly increased**, RR 1.63. Not on the WHO EML.
- **Complication: UCG 2023 still carries it** (30 mg/kg IV, child 25–50 mg/kg,
  then 8 mg/kg/h), gated behind "only if not responding to adequate atropine".
- **ASK:** omit silently, or omit with a footnote noting the UCG divergence?

## 1.4 🟡 Flumazenil: gated on the algorithm, or off it entirely?
- **OE:** add contraindications, keep for confirmed isolated ingestion. **← APPLIED**
- **Research:** remove from the overdose algorithm **entirely**; confine to
  procedural-sedation reversal. Notes our printed doses *are* the conscious-
  sedation doses; not on the WHO EML; Penninga 2016 (13 RCTs, 994 patients)
  adverse events **RR 2.85**, serious **RR 3.81**, mainly fits and arrhythmias.
- **ASK:** should it appear on a general overdose algorithm at all?

## 1.5 🔴 Phenobarbital loading in non-neonates: 20 or 15 mg/kg?
- **OE:** 15 → **20 mg/kg** for all children. **← APPLIED**
- **Research: KEEP 15.** WHO Pocket Book prints 15 in **three** places (Chart 9
  p.14; Annex 2 p.343; pp.49–50); **UCG 2023 prints 10–15**; AES 2016 prints 15.
  FDA (15–20) and ERC (20) are where 20 comes from.
- **Practical argument for 15:** same destination via an explicit repeat pathway,
  less first-pass apnoea where there is no ventilator. (Cape Town paediatric
  series: respiratory depression drove 65.5% of PICU admissions.)
- **Missing either way:** repeat **10 mg/kg to a cumulative max 40 mg/kg**.
- **Maintenance:** we print 3–5; better-sourced is **2.5–5 mg/kg/day**.
- **ASK:** 15 + repeat-to-40, or flat 20?

## 1.6 🔴 Paediatric diazepam maximum: 8 mg or 10 mg?
- **OE:** 0.2 mg/kg, **max 8 mg** (FDA). **← APPLIED** across pb_020, pb_042 ×2, pb_029.
- **Research:** cap should be **10 mg**. The FDA's 8 mg is an **artefact of its own
  regimen** (0.2 mg/kg × 40 kg), not a safety ceiling. **UCG 2023 (0.2 mg/kg, max
  10 mg)**, AES 2016, ERC 2021, MSF all print 10. The cap only bites above ~40 kg.
- **Missing:** MSF and others print **"maximum 2 doses"** before phenobarbital.
- **⚠️ Our original 0.3 was not an error.** WHO Annex 2 p.336 prints
  **"IV: 0.2–0.3 mg/kg"** and Chart 9 p.14 prints 0.25. WHO gives a band and we
  had printed its top. Moving to 0.2 is still defensible (bottom of the band,
  matches UCG) but "0.3 was wrong" was too strong, and the p.29 "inconsistency"
  was two ends of one band, not a contradiction.
- **ASK:** 8 or 10 mg?

## 1.7 🔴 Famotidine in anaphylaxis: correct as printed, or delete the drug?
- **OE:** confirmed correct as printed, no change. **← APPLIED** (0.25 mg/kg IV
  max 20 mg / 0.5 mg/kg PO max 40 mg)
- **Research: delete the H2 blocker entirely.** No H2 blocker appears in RCUK
  2021, the AAAAI/ACAAI 2020 or 2023 parameters, **WHO EML §3 (antiallergics and
  medicines used in anaphylaxis)**, or **UCG 2023 §1.1.1 anaphylactic shock**.
  RCUK verbatim: *"There is no evidence to support the routine use of an
  H2-receptor antihistamine … to treat anaphylaxis."* Nurmatov 2014 systematic
  review: *"failed to identify any eligible studies for inclusion"* — zero RCTs.
- **If kept anyway:** the PO cap should come down — **0.5 mg/kg IV slow over
  ≥2 min or PO, max 20 mg both routes; adult 20 mg**. Our 40 mg PO cap traces to
  a single US institutional pathway.
- **ASK:** delete, or keep as an optional post-adrenaline adjunct at 20 mg cap?

## 1.8 🔴 Anaphylaxis observation: ≥4 h, or ≥6 h with children admitted?
- **OE:** ≥4 h after last epinephrine; ≥6 h if severe/repeat-dosed. **← APPLIED**
- **Research: 6 h minimum for anyone who got adrenaline; 12 h/overnight if
  severe; admit all children under 16.** Grounded in NICE NG258 (May 2026):
  - the **2-hour fast-track requires the patient to already hold two in-date
    adrenaline auto-injectors** — structurally impossible in Uganda, so no
    patient qualifies;
  - the **12-hour criteria include "presents out-of-hours" and "would be
    discharged to a geographical area where access to emergency care is
    difficult"** — routinely true for a rural catchment;
  - **1.1.11:** admit under-16s who cannot be discharged under the fast-track.
- Biphasic reactions ~4.6–5%, **median time to second phase ~12 h**; RCUK notes a
  6–12 h rule "may miss over 50%".
- **Genuine guideline split:** US guidance (AAAAI 2023) is moving *shorter*
  (1–4 h, risk-stratified); UK/EU keeps 6–12–24 h tiers. What decides it here is
  logistics, not biology — and NICE says so explicitly.
- **ASK:** for a rural district hospital with no auto-injectors, is 6 h / 12 h /
  admit-under-16 right?

## 1.9 🟡 Doxycycline age restriction in bite prophylaxis
- **APPLIED:** "children ≥ 8 yrs 2.2 mg/kg/dose PO BD".
- **Research: doxycycline is safe at ANY age for courses ≤21 days.** AAP Red Book
  (2018 onward) verbatim: *"doxycycline can be used for short durations (ie, 21
  days or less) without regard to patient age"*; CDC recommends it for children of
  all ages in rickettsial disease; MSF says the same; a 34-study systematic review
  found no association with tooth staining. NICE's 12-year cut-off reflects old
  labelling, not data. **So our age restriction is over-restrictive and would
  push a Ugandan clinician to a worse regimen in a young child.**
- **ASK:** drop the age restriction (with the ≤21-day caveat)?

## 2.1 Methanol — full antidote/dialysis block (nothing applied)
- **Folinic acid 1 mg/kg (max 50 mg) IV q4h** in 5% dextrose over 30–60 min,
  ≥24–48 h. If unavailable, **folic acid at the same dose** PO/IV q4–6h.
  **Repeat after every dialysis session** (folate is dialysed out).
- **Dialysis criteria** (any one): new visual loss · coma/fits · pH ≤7.15 or
  HCO₃ <10 · acidosis persisting despite bicarbonate + ethanol · >30 mL (or
  >1 g/kg) ingested · methanol >50 mg/dL · renal failure.
- **⚠️ Ethanol maintenance should be DOUBLED in regular drinkers** and during
  dialysis. Under-dosing is called the commonest error — and regular drinkers are
  exactly who drinks bad waragi. Our 0.2–0.4 mL/kg/h band is correct but unflagged.
- **No dialysis:** continue ethanol **5–7 days**; bicarbonate mmol = 0.3 × wt ×
  base deficit.
- **Gulu Regional Referral Hospital is itself a dialysis centre** — this turns
  "refer for dialysis" into a concrete instruction.
- Fomepizole one-liner "where available"; **never together with ethanol**.
- **Honest caveat:** human efficacy data for folate in methanol poisoning are
  essentially absent (primate data are good). Recommended on plausibility, safety
  and cost — not on trial evidence.
- **ASK:** confirm folinic/folic dose, dialysis criteria, regular-drinker adjustment.

## 2.2 Activated charcoal — window refinement and the missing dose
- **APPLIED:** "within 2 h (up to 4 h for a large ingestion)".
- **Research** (Clinical Toxicology Recommendations Collaborative 2026, first full
  revision since the 1990s/2000s): **≤2 h strong, ≤3 h conditional, suggest
  against beyond 6 h.** The 4 h figure belongs specifically to **massive (≥30 g)**
  ingestion; modified-release paracetamol extends to 5–6 h.
- **We print no charcoal DOSE anywhere** — CTRC 2026: **50 g adult, 1 g/kg child**.
- Two safety lines: never intubate solely to give charcoal; if not fully awake,
  do not give it.
- **ASK:** confirm CTRC windows and the dose.

## 2.3 Snakebite — neostigmine trial (nothing applied)
WHO AFRO 2010 §15.4 answers this directly and **does recommend it for Africa**,
naming African species in which it worked:
- **Atropine FIRST, always:** adult **0.6 mg slow IV**, child **50 µg/kg slow IV**.
- **Then neostigmine IM:** adult **0.02 mg/kg**, child **0.04 mg/kg**.
  (Or edrophonium adult 10 mg / child 0.25 mg/kg slow IV.)
- **If positive:** neostigmine adult **0.5–2.5 mg q1–3 h (max 10 mg/24 h)**, child
  **0.01–0.04 mg/kg q2–4 h**, each dose with atropine. Stop after 3 doses if no
  response. Judge on ptosis/muscle power at 30–60 min (~≥50% ptosis recovery).
- **🔴 EXCLUDE MAMBAS** — mamba venom contains its own anticholinesterase
  (fasciculin). **This matters a lot in Uganda: UCG 2023 lists black mamba and
  Jameson's mamba among the commonest neurotoxic snakes**, alongside Egyptian and
  forest cobra.
- **Three guidelines print materially different regimens** — WHO AFRO (above),
  South African SASS 2023 (glycopyrrolate 0.6 mg IV + neostigmine 2.5 mg IV q30
  min, max 10 mg, adult-only), US JTS CPG (atropine 0.5 mg + neostigmine 1.0 mg).
  Recommendation: use WHO AFRO — Africa-specific, weight-based, and works with
  atropine, which is on the WHO EML.
- **Weakest evidence base in this whole document:** no RCTs; efficacy expected
  only against post-synaptic (cobra) blockade; no role in pre-synaptic envenoming.
  WHO's own text is internally inconsistent (cites success in a green mamba case,
  then excludes mambas on theoretical grounds).
- **ASK:** confirm the WHO AFRO regimen and the mamba exclusion.

## 2.4 Rabies immunoglobulin — further 2018 changes beyond what was applied
- **APPLIED:** infiltrate all into the wound, IM remainder only if the wound
  cannot take the volume.
- **Research says the remainder rule is stronger than that.** WHO 2018 verbatim:
  the remainder *"does not need to be injected IM at a distance from the wound but
  can be fractionated in smaller, individual syringes to be used for other
  patients."* i.e. leftover RIG is **saved for another patient**, not given IM.
- **Dose is a MAXIMUM, not a target:** *"The maximum dose is 20 IU (hRIG) and
  40 IU (eRIG) per kg … There is no minimum dose."* Our "total dose given once"
  now reads as a target.
- Also: **category III only** (plus category II if immunodeficient); **no eRIG
  skin test** (*"should be abandoned"*); **dilute with sterile saline** for small
  children or multiple wounds; never after day 7 of the first vaccine dose; never
  withhold vaccine for lack of RIG.
- **⚠️ UCG 2023 still prints the pre-2018 instruction** (*"Give the remainder IM
  into gluteal muscle"*). Printing WHO 2018 diverges from the national guideline
  on the next shelf — defensible, but the author should know, and a footnote
  would stop a clinician being blindsided.
- **Vaccine days 0/3/7/14 IM confirmed still correct.**
- **ASK:** confirm the "save it for another patient" framing and the maximum-not-
  target reading.

## 2.5 Snakebite — the IM rule is broader than tetanus
- **APPLIED:** tetanus toxoid, deferring IM while coagulopathic.
- **Research:** WHO SEARO's instruction is general — *"strict bed rest to avoid
  even minor trauma including intramuscular injections"* — so it applies to
  **every** IM drug, not just tetanus. Use IV or oral while clotting is abnormal.
  Practical trigger: the **20WBCT**, which UCG 2023 already prescribes for
  snakebite; clotting typically returns 3–9 h after adequate antivenom, so
  re-test at 6 h.
- **Honest note:** no source says "delay the tetanus toxoid" in those words. It
  is a correct inference from an explicit WHO instruction, not a quoted rule.

# 3. RAISED BY RESEARCH, NEVER PUT TO OE

## 3.1 🔴 Folic acid + antifolate antimalarials — a real interaction
Cochrane, **February 2026** (8 trials, 3,486 participants): in people on antifolate
antimalarials, folic acid **reduced day-3 parasite clearance** (RR 0.89) and
**increased treatment failure** at days 7/14/28 (RR 2.12 / 1.97 / 1.35), moderate
certainty. **Dose-dependent** — the effect appears above 1 mg; the single 400 µg
trial showed little to none. Ouma RCT: day-14 failure 13.9% placebo / 14.5% at
0.4 mg / **27.1% at 5 mg**.
We pair iron with folate by default, and **cotrimoxazole prophylaxis is widespread
in Ugandan paediatrics — trimethoprim is an antifolate too**. MSF: antifolate-
induced anaemia is treated with *folinic* acid, not folic acid.
**ASK:** drop routine folate with iron, cap at 400 µg, or carry a "never 5 mg with
SP or cotrimoxazole" caveat?

## 3.2 🔴 Post-discharge malaria chemoprevention (PDMC) is absent from the book
Full ACT course at discharge, 1 month, 2 months after severe anaemia.
Kwambai *NEJM* 2020 + pooled trials: **all-cause mortality −77%, readmissions
−55% at 6 months.** WHO added it June 2022. Argued to be the strongest mortality
evidence of anything in the anaemia section — and it aligns follow-up visits with
the delayed-haemolysis monitoring window.
**ASK:** confirm PDMC and its regimen for this setting.

## 3.3 🟡 Post-artesunate delayed haemolysis — trigger and lab panel
**APPLIED:** weekly CBC + LDH/bilirubin/haptoglobin/reticulocytes × 4 weeks for
all children treated for severe malaria.
Research: interval and duration right (WHO 4–6 wk; CDC 4 wk), but the trigger
should be **"received parenteral artesunate"**, and the four-marker panel weekly
is **not obtainable at a Ugandan district hospital** — mandating it turns a
life-saving instruction into a dead letter, when **haemoglobin plus a clinical
look** is what drives the decision to transfuse. Incidence in African children is
**~23%** (Carret, *CID* 2025), not the ~5% often assumed, so restricting to
hyperparasitaemia would miss most cases. Days 14 and 21 are highest-yield.
**ASK:** confirm trigger; downgrade the marker panel to "if available"?

## 3.4 🟡 Anaemia treatment threshold Hgb < 9
Below WHO's 2024 thresholds (10.5 g/dL at 6–23 mo, 11.0 at 24–59 mo, 11.5 at
5–11 y). Probably a deliberate rationing choice — but currently an inherited
number rather than a decided one.
**ASK:** deliberate, or should it move?

## 3.5 🟡 Bite prophylaxis — indications, and a no-co-amoxiclav fallback
Neither OE nor our page states **when** to give bite prophylaxis. UCG 2023 and
IDSA agree on nearly the same list: deep puncture (esp. cat) · human bite ·
severe/deep/extensive · face, genitalia or hands · over a joint or bone ·
immunocompromised/HIV, diabetes, asplenia · already infected — i.e. **not routine
for a small uncomplicated dog bite**. UCG also gives a fallback for where
co-amoxiclav is not stocked (amoxicillin + metronidazole, 5–7 days), which is the
realistic district-level situation.
**ASK:** add indications and the amoxicillin + metronidazole fallback?

# 4. NO RE-QUERY NEEDED — resolved, recorded for completeness

- **Dextrose arithmetic.** The 8:1 correction was independently confirmed as
  **exactly D10.00%**. Bonus finding: **WHO's own alternative (1 part D50 to 9
  parts D5) computes to 9.5%**, not 10%. WHO's water version (1 part D50 + 4 parts
  water) is exact and is what UCG 2023 prints. Suggested: print all three exact
  options and warn that **1 part D50 into 4 parts D5 gives 14%** — the failure
  mode is substituting D5 where the recipe says water. Also confirmed: 5 mL/kg of
  D10 (0.5 g/kg) is the WHO figure and modal practice (81% of surveyed EPs),
  defensible range 0.2–1 g/kg.
- **Epinephrine max 1 mg** — confirmed by both. Suggested addition: a decimal-error
  guard ("if your calculated dose exceeds 1 mg / 10 mL, recheck the decimal").
- **Organophosphate diazepam dose** — both agree: do **not** reduce it. Research
  adds that OP status becomes **benzodiazepine-refractory** as GABA-A receptors
  internalise, so under-dosing is the documented failure mode; the OP-specific
  caution is **airway**, not dose.
- **IO insertion angle 90°** — both agree it is a fine simplification.
