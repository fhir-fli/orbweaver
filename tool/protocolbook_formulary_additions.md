# ProtocolBook formulary — drugs added beyond the printed Essential Medications list

**52 STILL NEED CHECKING.** Three have been checked already and are excluded from the
list below: meropenem and vancomycin came back **wrong** and are corrected; naloxone
came back correct.

The printed list (pp.146–158) carries 84 drugs. The protocols prescribe more. 18 were
added on 2026-08-27 from the drugs the corrected chapters used; 37 more on 2026-08-29
by asking which drugs ANY protocol in the book prescribes. That found labetalol — the
hypertension page's own first choice in emergency — and acetylcysteine, the paracetamol
antidote, both absent.

**Every dose is taken from the page that already prescribes it,** so nothing here is a
new clinical claim. **That is exactly why it needs checking:** these doses were
reviewed inside their protocol, and a formulary entry is read without that context.
Meropenem and vancomycin are what that looks like — both were the generic dose where
the entry named an intracranial indication, and both were wrong on the page too.

Formulary now holds 139 drugs.

## Activated Charcoal

*antidote*

**Single dose 1 g/kg PO or by NGT, within 1 hour of ingestion** (up to 4 hrs for a large ingestion), for substances known to be adsorbed.

Do **not** give to a patient who is not fully awake, and **never intubate solely in order to give it**.

## Adenosine

*antiarrhythmic*

**Stable** regular narrow-complex SVT — after vagal manoeuvres.

**Children:** 0.1 mg/kg rapid IV push (max 6 mg); if no response 0.2 mg/kg (max 12 mg)

**Adults:** 6 mg rapid IV push + 20 mL flush; if no response 12 mg; may repeat 12 mg. **Max 3 doses**

## Alteplase (rt-PA)

*thrombolytic*

**High-risk (massive) PE — adults:** **100 mg IV over 2 hours**
- Peri-arrest / cardiac arrest: **0.6 mg/kg over ~15 min (max 50 mg)**
- A 50 mg regimen has similar efficacy with less bleeding (evidence for full equivalence is limited)

⚠️ **The PE regimen is NOT the STEMI regimen** — do not carry a STEMI infusion schedule across.

## Apixaban

*anticoagulant*

**VTE treatment — adults:** 10 mg PO BD × 7 days, then **5 mg PO BD**
- Extended prophylaxis after ≥ 6 months, if continued: 2.5 mg BD
- **No parenteral lead-in required**

**Children:** specialist-directed only

## Artemether

*antimalarial*

**Severe malaria, 2nd line — only if artesunate is unavailable.**

- **3.2 mg/kg IM** on admission
- then **1.6 mg/kg IM daily** until able to take oral → switch to a full oral ACT course

## Aspirin

*antiplatelet*

- **ACS: 300–325 mg PO × 1 dose**, chewed, as early as possible
- **Ischaemic stroke: 300 mg PO stat**, then **75–100 mg daily**
- **Acute rheumatic fever arthritis: 20–25 mg/kg/dose PO q6h**

## Atropine

*antidote*

**Organophosphate / carbamate poisoning — titrate to atropinisation.**

- **Children 0.02 mg/kg IV · Adults 2 mg IV**, then **DOUBLE the previous dose every 5 min** until the endpoints are met
- **Endpoints:** chest clear of secretions, pulse > 80/min, SBP > 80 mmHg
- **Maintenance:** mix the total bolus dose into NS or D5% — < 25 kg 250 mL at 50 mL/hr; > 25 kg 500 mL at 100 mL/hr; adjust hourly

**Snake bite, before neostigmine:** adults 0.6 mg slow IV; children 50 mcg/kg slow IV

## Calcium Gluconate 10%

*electrolyte*

**Hyperkalaemia with ECG changes.**

**Children:** 0.5 mL/kg slow IV over 2–3 min with cardiac monitoring; repeat every 5 min until the ECG improves

**Adults:** 10 mL of 10% IV over 2–3 min; repeat every 5 min until the ECG improves

## Cefixime

*antibiotic*

**Gonorrhoea — only where ceftriaxone is unavailable.**

**Adults:** 800 mg PO single dose (urogenital or rectal only)

**Children:** 8 mg/kg PO as a **single dose**; ≥ 45 kg use the adult 800 mg single dose

## Clindamycin

*antibiotic*

- **Children: 7 mg/kg/dose PO 8-hourly (max 300 mg)** × 10 days — streptococcal pharyngitis in penicillin allergy
- **Adults: 600 mg IV 8-hourly** — sepsis; also covers anaerobes

## Clopidogrel

*antiplatelet*

**ACS with fibrinolysis:**
- **Age ≤ 75: 300 mg PO loading, then 75 mg daily**
- **Age > 75: NO loading dose — 75 mg daily** (excess intracranial haemorrhage)

**ACS without fibrinolysis:** 300–600 mg loading, then 75 mg daily

## Dapagliflozin

*cardiac*

**Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction — 10 mg PO once daily, where available.**

A fourth pillar alongside an ACE-inhibitor, a beta-blocker and spironolactone.

## Diphenhydramine

*antihistamine*

**Anaphylaxis adjunct only — 1 mg/kg IV or PO, max 50 mg.**

🛑 Does **not** treat airway, breathing or circulation. **Never let it delay or replace epinephrine.**

## Dobutamine

*vasoactive*

**Pump failure with volume overload / cardiogenic shock.**

**Children and adults:** 2–20 mcg/kg/min IV, titrated — often combined with
norepinephrine when the patient is hypotensive

## Dopamine

*vasoactive*

🛑 **Fallback only — use norepinephrine first.**

**Children and adults:** 5–20 mcg/kg/min IV

⚠️ 2–5 mcg/kg/min is the old "renal dose" and is **not** a pressor dose — do not
rely on it for hypotension. **Always per kilogram.**

## Doxycycline

*antibiotic*

**Chlamydia — adults:** 100 mg PO BD × 7 days (21 days for LGV / proctitis)

**Pelvic inflammatory disease — adults:** 100 mg PO/IV BD × 14 days, with
ceftriaxone + metronidazole

**Children ≥ 8 y or > 45 kg:** 100 mg PO BD. Under 8 y — prefer azithromycin
where an alternative exists

## Empagliflozin

*cardiac*

**Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction — 10 mg PO once daily, where available.**

A fourth pillar alongside an ACE-inhibitor, a beta-blocker and spironolactone.

## Famotidine

*gastrointestinal*

**20 mg IV 12-hourly** — where a proton-pump inhibitor is not available. A PPI is first line in upper GI bleeding.

## Flucloxacillin

*antibiotic*

**50 mg/kg IV 6-hourly** — staphylococcal cover, e.g. mastoiditis. Use cloxacillin **or** flucloxacillin, not both.

## Flucytosine (5-FC)

*antifungal*

**Cryptococcal meningitis induction**, with amphotericin B and fluconazole.

**Children and adults:** **25 mg/kg/dose PO four times daily (100 mg/kg/day)**

Duration: **14 days** with the single-dose liposomal amphotericin B 10 mg/kg
regimen, or **7 days** with the amphotericin B deoxycholate 1-week regimen

## Haloperidol

*psychiatric*

**Acute agitation — 2.5–5 mg IV/IM**, alone or with midazolam 2.5–5 mg.

Olanzapine 10 mg IM, where stocked, has fewer extrapyramidal effects.

## Hydrocortisone

*steroid*

- **Anaphylaxis adjunct: 2–4 mg/kg IM or slow IV, max 100 mg in a child (200 mg adult).** An adjunct of unproven benefit — it must never delay epinephrine
- **Paediatric septic shock: 2 mg/kg IV bolus, then 1 mg/kg IV q6h.** Do **not** use routinely

## Hypertonic Saline 3%

*fluid*

**Severely symptomatic hyponatraemia — bolus, not a slow infusion.**

- **Children: 4 mL/kg (max 100 mL) · Adults: 100 mL**, repeat until symptoms improve
- Predictive formulas systematically underestimate the rise and **must never replace serial sodium measurement**

Also used for raised intracranial pressure as an alternative to mannitol.

## Insulin (soluble / regular)

*endocrine*

**DKA — children:** 0.05–0.1 unit/kg/hr IV infusion (**no bolus in children**)
**DKA — adults:** 0.1 unit/kg/hr IV infusion (a 0.1 unit/kg bolus is optional)
- Once glucose ~11–14 mmol/L, **reduce to 0.02–0.05 unit/kg/hr and add dextrose**; continue until the anion gap closes

**Hyperkalaemia — children:** 0.05–0.1 unit/kg IV with glucose 0.5 g/kg
**Hyperkalaemia — adults:** 10 units IV with 25 g glucose (50 mL of 50%)

## Ipratropium

*respiratory*

**0.5 mg nebulised with salbutamol, every 20 min × 3 doses**, or 8 puffs by MDI with spacer.

## Itraconazole

*antifungal*

**Disseminated histoplasmosis.**

**Adults:** 200 mg PO TID × 3 days (loading), then **200 mg PO BD**
**Children:** 5 mg/kg/dose PO TID × 3 days, then 5 mg/kg/dose BD (max 400 mg/day)

Duration **≥ 12 months** — stop only with > 12 months of therapy, ≥ 6 months on ART
with **CD4 ≥ 150**, negative fungal cultures, undetectable viral load, and
*Histoplasma* antigen below threshold

## Labetalol

*antihypertensive*

**First choice in hypertensive emergency here — it needs no infusion pump.**

- **10–20 mg IV** over 1–2 min, then **20–80 mg q10min** until at target, to a **300 mg** total
- **Oral: 200 mg PO, then 200–400 mg q6–12h PRN**
- **Acute stroke:** 10–20 mg IV over 1–2 min, may repeat once; lower BP by no more than 15% in the first 24 hrs
- **Aortic dissection:** give the beta-blocker BEFORE any vasodilator

## Magnesium Sulfate

*electrolyte*

**Symptomatic hypomagnesaemia — children:** 25–50 mg/kg IV (max 2 g) over 15–60 min
**Symptomatic hypomagnesaemia — adults:** 1–2 g IV over 15–60 min, then a slower
continuous infusion

**Severe asthma — children:** 25–50 mg/kg IV (max 2 g) over 20 min
**Severe asthma — adults:** 1.2–2 g IV over 20 min, single dose

**Eclampsia — adults:** loading 4–6 g IV over 15–20 min, then 1–2 g/hr; continue
24 h after the last seizure or delivery

## Mannitol

*neurology*

**Raised intracranial pressure — 0.5–1 g/kg IV.** Hypertonic saline is the alternative.

## Metformin

*endocrine*

**500 mg PO BD with meals**, once the patient is eating and DKA has resolved.

## Metoprolol

*cardiac*

- **Metoprolol tartrate 25 mg PO BD**
- **Metoprolol succinate (ER) 25 mg PO daily**

Post-ACS. Do not start a beta-blocker in cardiogenic shock or acute decompensated heart failure.

## Midazolam

*benzodiazepine*

- **Status epilepticus, no IV access: 10 mg IM** — as effective as IV lorazepam and faster to give
- **Buccal or intranasal: 0.3–0.5 mg/kg (max 10 mg)** — more effective than rectal diazepam
- **Acute agitation: 2.5–5 mg IV/IM**, alone or with haloperidol

## N-acetylcysteine (NAC)

*antidote*

**Paracetamol poisoning — two-bag, all weights down to 5 kg. Total 300mg/kg over 20 hrs.**

- **Child ≤ 40kg:** 200mg/kg in 7mL/kg diluent (max 500mL) over 4 hrs, then 100mg/kg in 14mL/kg (max 1L) over 16 hrs
- **> 40kg:** 200mg/kg (max 22g) in 500mL over 4 hrs, then 100mg/kg (max 11g) in 1L over 16 hrs
- Diluent D5W, ½NS or sterile water. If wt > 100kg, dose as 100kg
- **Under 5kg:** take toxicology advice

## Nicardipine

*antihypertensive*

- **IV, hypertensive emergency: 5 mg/hr initially**, increase 2.5 mg/hr q5min to a **maximum 15 mg/hr**. Needs an infusion pump
- **Oral: 20–40 mg PO q8–12h**

## Norepinephrine (Noradrenaline)

*vasoactive*

**First-line vasopressor** for septic shock, cardiogenic shock, and hypotension in
acute hyponatraemia.

**Children:** 0.05–0.1 mcg/kg/min IV, titrated (up to ~1 mcg/kg/min)
**Adults:** 0.05–0.5 mcg/kg/min IV, titrated to MAP ≥ 65 (up to 1 mcg/kg/min)

## Norfloxacin

*antibiotic*

**Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis prophylaxis — 400 mg PO daily, indefinitely.**

Co-trimoxazole 160/800 mg PO five days a week is the alternative.

## Octreotide

*gastrointestinal*

**Variceal bleeding — the vasoactive drug of choice. Start as soon as varices are suspected, before endoscopy.**

**50 mcg IV bolus, then 50 mcg/hr infusion for 3–5 days.**

## Olanzapine

*psychiatric*

**Acute agitation — 10 mg IM**, where stocked. Fewer extrapyramidal effects than haloperidol.

## Packed Red Blood Cells (PRBCs)

*blood product*

**10 mL/kg raises a child's haemoglobin about 1 g/dL** (10–15 mL/kg raises it 2–3 g/dL). One adult unit raises it about 1 g/dL.

Volumes by fever and nutrition are on the transfusion page — take the first line that applies.

## Penicillin V (phenoxymethylpenicillin)

*antibiotic*

- **Streptococcal pharyngitis: children 250 mg PO BD × 10 days; adults 500 mg PO BD × 10 days**
- **Strep eradication: 250 mg PO TID**
- **Rheumatic fever secondary prophylaxis: 250 mg PO BD**

Oral ampicillin is **not** a substitute — it is poorly and erratically absorbed.

## Phenylephrine

*vasopressor*

**Priapism, if aspiration alone fails — 100–500 mcg intracavernosal**, repeated every 5 min to a **maximum 1 mg total**.

Dilute 1 mg in 19 mL NS to give 50 mcg/mL.

## Potassium Chloride (KCl)

*electrolyte*

- **DKA, K⁺ 3.3–5.3 with urine output established: 10 mEq per 500 mL bag (= 20 mmol/L).** The usual case
- **Severe acute malnutrition: add 20 mmol/L** to the maintenance fluid

🛑 Peripheral ceiling 40 mmol/L. Do not exceed 10 mmol/hr without continuous ECG.

## Rabies Immunoglobulin

*biological*

**Category III exposure only. Infiltrate as much as possible around the wound.**

- **Human Ig 20 IU/kg · Equine Ig 40 IU/kg**

🛑 This is a **maximum, not a target** — there is no minimum dose. Exceeding it suppresses the vaccine response.

## Rifaximin

*gastrointestinal*

**Recurrent hepatic encephalopathy — add to lactulose**, which stays first line at 30–45 mL PO every 1–2 hrs until two bowel movements, then 15–30 mL 2–4 times daily.

## Rivaroxaban

*anticoagulant*

**VTE treatment — adults:** 15 mg PO BD × 21 days, then **20 mg PO once daily
(with food)**
- **No parenteral lead-in required**

**Children:** specialist-directed only

## Sodium Nitroprusside

*antihypertensive*

**0.3–0.5 mcg/kg/min**, increasing 0.5 mcg/kg/min every few minutes, to a **maximum 10 mcg/kg/min**.

🛑 Avoid in hypertensive encephalopathy. Cyanide toxicity with prolonged or high-dose use.

## Streptokinase

*thrombolytic*

**STEMI — adults:** 1.5 million IU IV over **60 minutes**

**High-risk PE — adults:** **250,000 IU IV over 30 min, then 100,000 IU/hr for
12–24 hours** (an accelerated 1.5 million IU over 2 h is also described)

⚠️ **The PE regimen is a prolonged 12–24 h infusion, unlike the single STEMI
infusion** — this is the key difference on the shelf.

## Tenecteplase

*thrombolytic*

**STEMI — adults**, single IV bolus over 5 seconds, by weight:
- < 60 kg: 30 mg · 60–69 kg: 35 mg · 70–79 kg: 40 mg · 80–89 kg: 45 mg · ≥ 90 kg: 50 mg

**High-risk PE — adults:** the **same weight-banded bolus** (off-label for PE)

## Terlipressin

*gastrointestinal*

**Variceal bleeding, if octreotide is unavailable — 2 mg IV q4–6h until bleeding stops, then 1 mg q4–6h.**

More adverse effects than octreotide; avoid in ischaemic heart disease.

## Ticagrelor

*antiplatelet*

**NSTEMI NOT receiving fibrinolysis — adults:** 180 mg PO loading, then
**90 mg PO BD** (60 mg BD after 12 months if continued)

## Tranexamic Acid (TXA)

*haemostatic*

**Epistaxis — 500 mg in 5 mL NS on a pledget**, left in place 10 minutes, as the first agent tried.

## Whole Blood (WB)

*blood product*

**20 mL/kg raises a child's haemoglobin about 1 g/dL**; one adult unit about 1 g/dL.

- **Septic shock not responding to the first bolus: 10 mL/kg**
- Volumes by fever and nutrition are on the transfusion page — take the first line that applies

10 mL PRBC ≈ 20 mL whole blood as a bedside rule; **do not** use it to derive the fever-stratified volumes.
