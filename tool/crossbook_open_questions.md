## 1 🔴 Transfusion volume in a child — should it be stratified by FEVER?

**One reference stratifies the volume by fever and gives a mortality reason:**
> **Febrile (T ≥ 37.5 °C): give the lower volume — whole blood 20 ml/kg (or packed cells
> 10 ml/kg) — higher volumes increased mortality in febrile children.**
> **Afebrile (T < 37.5 °C): give the higher volume — whole blood 30 ml/kg (or packed
> cells 20 ml/kg) — it lowers mortality.**

**The other reference stratifies by age and by malnutrition, and says nothing about
fever:**
> 10 mL PRBCs = 20 mL whole blood. If in shock, give WB for extra volume. If no shock and
> concern for volume overload (malnourished, heart failure), give PRBCs.
> **< 1 yr:** 10 mL/kg PRBCs, or 15 mL/kg WB
> **> 1 yr:** 10 mL/kg PRBCs, or 20 mL/kg WB
> **Malnourished:** 5 mL/kg PRBCs, or 10 mL/kg WB (do not repeat the transfusion)
> Usual range for children: 10–20 mL/kg. Usually run 10 mL/kg (1 unit) over 3–4 hours.

**The concern.** In a malaria-endemic paediatric hospital a large share of transfused
children are febrile. If the fever rule is real, the second reference gives some of them
a volume associated with higher mortality, and gives afebrile children less than the
volume associated with lower mortality. But adding a mortality claim to a book that does
not carry it should not be done on the strength of the other book alone.

**Questions.** Please answer each on its own line, with no citation after the number.
1. Should paediatric transfusion volume be **stratified by fever**? Yes or no.
2. If yes, give the **febrile** and **afebrile** volumes for **whole blood** and for
   **packed cells**, and the temperature threshold.
3. Does the fever rule **replace** the age and malnutrition stratification, or sit
   alongside it? A malnourished febrile child would otherwise have two different answers.
4. What is the evidence, and does it hold outside the trial population?
