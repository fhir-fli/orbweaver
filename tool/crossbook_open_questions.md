## 1 🔴 Adrenaline in adult cardiac arrest — every 2 minutes, or every 3–5?

One reference draws its adult cardiac-arrest algorithm as a two-minute cycle, and puts
adrenaline inside the cycle node:

> **Every 2 minutes →** Change compressors · Check pulse ≤ 10 sec · **Epinephrine 1 mg IV
> in 10 ml normal saline, followed by 20 ml normal saline flush**

Read as drawn, that gives **1 mg every 2 minutes**.

The **paediatric** page of the **same** reference prints:
> **Repeat epinephrine every 3–5 minutes**

**What was done.** The adult chart now states **every 3–5 minutes**, explicitly separated
from the two-minute compressor-change and rhythm-check cycle, on the grounds that the
same book already prints 3–5 minutes for children. **Please confirm or correct** — it is
a patient-safety change made without an adjudication.

**Questions.** Each figure on its own line, with no citation after the number.
1. In **adult** cardiac arrest, what is the correct adrenaline **interval**?
2. In a **child**, what is the interval?
3. Is there any circumstance in which adrenaline every 2 minutes is correct in arrest?
4. Does the interval differ between **shockable and non-shockable** rhythms, or by when
   the first dose is given?
