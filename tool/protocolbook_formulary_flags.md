# ProtocolBook formulary (pp.146–158) — advance pass

Raised 2026-08-27, after the pp.12–142 chapter corrections were applied. The method here
is the one that has found the worst errors before: **compare the book against itself.**
Fourteen of the formulary's 84 drugs touch something the chapters changed this month, so
the formulary was checked entry-by-entry against the corrected chapter doses.

**Six contradictions were already settled by OE's chapter answers and have been applied**
(see the table at the bottom). **Two findings are new and need OE.**

---

# 🔴 F-1 The valaciclovir and aciclovir HSV doses appear to be crossed

**Verbatim, as printed.**

| | Aciclovir (p.146) | Valaciclovir (p.156) |
|---|---|---|
| **Children** | HSV **20 mg/kg/dose PO TID** · VZV 20 mg/kg/dose PO QID | HSV **1 g PO BD** · VZV 20 mg/kg/dose PO TID |
| **Adults** | HSV **400 mg PO TID** · VZV 1 g PO TID | HSV **400 mg PO TID** · VZV 1 g PO TID |

**The concern.** Two things look wrong, and they look like the same error.

1. The **adult HSV dose is identical for both drugs — 400 mg PO TID.** 400 mg three times
   daily is the dose I associate with **aciclovir**, and the two drugs are not
   interchangeable at the same dose (this book has just corrected the reverse error on the
   zoster page, where aciclovir had been given valaciclovir's 1 g TID).
2. The **paediatric valaciclovir HSV dose is "1 g PO BD"** — a flat gram twice daily, with
   no per-kilogram term, in the children's row. That reads like an **adult** dose sitting
   in the child column, while the adult row holds what looks like aciclovir's dose. The
   two rows may simply be swapped.

**Questions.**
1. What is the correct **adult** valaciclovir dose for genital HSV — initial episode and
   recurrence? Please give each on its own line.
2. What is the correct **paediatric** valaciclovir dose for HSV, in mg/kg? Standalone line.
3. Is "400 mg PO TID" a valaciclovir dose for any indication, or is it aciclovir's dose
   printed in the wrong entry?
4. Please also confirm the **adult valaciclovir dose for zoster** — the book prints 1 g TID
   for both drugs, and only one of them should be that.

# 🟡 F-2 Eighteen drugs the protocols prescribe are not in the formulary at all

**The finding.** The formulary is the drug reference this app offers, and a whole-book
check found **18 drugs that the protocol pages give dosing for but that have no formulary
entry**:

**flucytosine · itraconazole · doxycycline · cefixime · clopidogrel · ticagrelor ·
tenecteplase · streptokinase · alteplase · adenosine · dopamine · norepinephrine ·
dobutamine · insulin · magnesium · calcium gluconate · rivaroxaban · apixaban**

Several of these are load-bearing for corrections just applied:
- **Flucytosine** — the cryptococcal induction regimen now on p.136 depends on it
- **Doxycycline** — now first-line for chlamydia
- **Tenecteplase, streptokinase, alteplase** — the STEMI and the new high-risk-PE pathways
- **Adenosine** — the corrected SVT algorithm
- **Calcium gluconate, insulin, salbutamol** — the corrected hyperkalaemia algorithm
- **Norepinephrine, dobutamine** — now the preferred pressors, replacing dopamine

**The question is one of scope, and it is Grey's, not mine.** The formulary reproduces the
printed "Essential Medications" list, so adding entries is a deliberate departure from the
print rather than a correction of it.

**Questions for Grey (not OE):**
1. Should the formulary be **extended beyond the print** to cover every drug the protocols
   prescribe? That is the reader's reasonable expectation — a dose given in a protocol
   should be findable in the drug list.
2. If yes, should the additions be marked as **not in the printed book**, the way other
   evidence-driven departures are?
3. If no, should the protocol pages instead carry a note that the formulary covers only
   the printed essential-medicines list?

# ✅ Applied — contradictions the chapter answers already settled

| Drug | Was | Now |
|---|---|---|
| **Aciclovir** p.146 | adult VZV **1 g PO TID** | **800 mg PO five times daily** — matches the corrected zoster entry; the two drugs are not interchangeable at one dose |
| **Warfarin** p.158 | adults **10 mg PO OD × 2 days** | **no loading dose**; start 5 mg, **2.5 mg or less if older/frail/malnourished/liver disease**; **VTE needs ≥5 days LMWH overlap and INR ≥2 for 24 h**; first INR at 3–5 days |
| **Enoxaparin** p.150 | one ACS dose, no age term | **age ≥ 75 with fibrinolysis: no IV bolus, 0.75 mg/kg q12h (max 75 mg ×2)** |
| **Rifabutin** p.156 | children treatment **15 mg/kg**, no cap | **10–20 mg/kg, maximum 300 mg/day** |
| **Amphotericin** p.147 | liposomal **4 mg/kg/day** only | **single 10 mg/kg dose** added as preferred for cryptococcal meningitis, with its flucytosine + fluconazole partners |
| **Potassium** p.155 | "1 mEq/kg/dose", no rate or concentration | **0.2 mEq/kg/hr unmonitored**; peripheral ≤40 mEq/L, central ≤400 under ICU care; **never bolus** |

# ✅ Checked and already consistent
- **Heparin** p.152 gives venous UFH as **80 units/kg then 18 units/kg/hr with no caps** —
  which is correct. **The formulary was right and the DVT chapter was wrong**; the chapter
  was corrected on 2026-08-27 to match. Worth recording: the cross-check runs both ways.
- **Ceftriaxone** p.148 adult "meningitis 2 g q12hr, other 2 g q24hr" — the earlier
  "q23hr" typo is fixed and the doses are standard. It carries **no gonorrhoea line**;
  the chapter's 500 mg IM covers it.
